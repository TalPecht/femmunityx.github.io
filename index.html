Our central theme

Female immunity is not a fixed state.

We study female immunity as a dynamic biological system shaped by endocrine signals, reproductive physiology, metabolism and environmental context. Rather than treating physiological variation as background noise, we ask how immune states change within women over time, which biological signals drive those changes, and when those dynamics become relevant for health and disease.

Flagship research initiative

The Femmunity Study

The Femmunity Study is a multidisciplinary human research initiative investigating how the menstrual cycle and hormonal contraceptives influence immune function, metabolism and gut-microbiome dynamics.

Its goal is to deepen our understanding of female physiology and identify immunological or molecular signals that may inform menstrual and immune health.

Scientific aims

Four questions connect the Femmunity Study.

01

How does immune function change across the menstrual cycle?

We characterize how natural hormonal fluctuations are associated with changes in immune activity in healthy women.

02

How does hormonal contraception affect immunity and metabolism?

We examine whether contraceptive hormone exposure is associated with changes in immune function, metabolic parameters or gut-microbiome composition.

03

Can menstrual fluid provide a window into endometrial and immune health?

We profile menstrual fluid for immunological and molecular signals that may reflect reproductive and systemic biology.

04

How do hormones, metabolism and the gut microbiome interact?

We study how cyclical hormonal changes coincide with metabolic pathways and microbiome composition.

Projects within the Femmunity Study

Three complementary windows into female immunity.

MHOON

Menstruation · Hormones · Ovulation · Immunity

Following immune physiology across the natural menstrual cycle.

MHOON follows healthy participants across approximately five to six weeks, with five morning study visits. Participants keep a cycle and lifestyle diary and wear continuous sensors for body temperature and glucose. At each visit, health status and body composition are assessed and blood is collected to profile immune and metabolic states.

The repeated-measures design allows us to connect natural hormonal and ovulatory dynamics with within-person changes in immunity and metabolism.

5 visits 5–6 weeks cycle diary continuous sensors blood profiling
MHOON-Pill

Hormonal contraception · Immunity · Metabolism

Comparing immune states during pill exposure and the pill-free interval.

MHOON-Pill studies participants using oral hormonal contraception across four morning visits: two during active pill intake and two during a pill-free phase.

Daily lifestyle tracking, continuous temperature and glucose sensing, body-composition measurements and blood profiling provide a multidimensional view of how contraceptive hormone states relate to immunity and metabolism.

4 visits pill phase pill-free phase daily diary continuous sensors
ENDOSCOP

ENDOmetrium · Single-cell · Omics · Profiling

Using menstrual fluid as a window into endometrial immune biology.

Menstrual fluid contains cells, hormones, proteins and immune factors, yet remains comparatively understudied. ENDOSCOP investigates the immunological and molecular composition of menstrual fluid in healthy participants.

By profiling these samples at cellular and molecular resolution, we aim to uncover signals that are difficult to access through peripheral blood alone and build a richer picture of endometrial and immune physiology.

Wider research program

Female immunity within broader human immune variation.

Our wider work asks how metabolism, environment, population context and computation can help explain differences in immune states across physiological conditions and between individuals.

Population systems immunology

TransMic / TransInf

These projects examine how nutrition, metabolism, microbiome composition and environmental context shape immune states across populations. FemmunityX focuses especially on sex-associated and physiology-associated differences within these systems.

Computational framework

InformatYX

InformatYX is our developing computational framework for physiology-aware immune analysis. It connects immune measurements with hormonal, metabolic, demographic and population variables to identify dynamic signatures that standard cross-sectional analyses can miss.
